\section*{Thesis}
The economics literature that justifies CBI contains a \textbf{circular loop}: it encodes a normative-distributional choice (price-stability priority / the conservative-banker default) into the \textit{definition and measurement} of independence --- via implicit modeling assumptions (single social objective function; distributional heterogeneity assumed away) and hardened in the de jure indices (objectives component) --- and that same literature is then cited as the warrant for the institutional arrangement. \textbf{Epistemic foreclosure} = within the frame of this literature, the goal question cannot be posed: to revise the goal is, by construction, to reduce measured independence. The literature needs revision to make the buried normative-distributional decisions explicit.

\subsection*{Scope (hard rules)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item This is a critique of the \textbf{economics literature}, on its own terms. Contribution = the precise internal critique the poli-sci literature never makes (it stays abstract; nobody grounds the critique \textit{in} the CBI literature itself).
    \item Epistemic foreclosure = \textbf{literature-level} (question closed within the frame). NOT a claim that democratic revision is politically impossible / that democracy has failed --- that's a separate paper, flagged not claimed.
    \item Democratic-foreclosure + finance-benefits claims already exist in poli-sci (Adolph, Wansleben, Lindblom\ldots) --- cited as established, not as the contribution.
    \item Finance = \textbf{noted-not-causal}. Posen: default ``resembles creditor prefs,'' not ``finance built it.''
    \item Don't claim conservative weighting is \textit{wrong} --- only a contestable choice mis-coded as technical.
    \item No hypocrisy / ``all officials understand'' claims. Structural/circular only.
    \item Concede insulation$\rightarrow$inflation (Binder, Drechsel); contest only the normative leap.
\end{itemize}

\textit{Framing:} poli-sci criticises the arrangement (abstractly, real-world-grounded) $\leftrightarrow$ economics defends it (conservative banker + indices) $\rightarrow$ I attack the economics side on its own terms.

\subsection{The Economics Literature on CBI (\textasciitilde700 words)}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Debelle \& Fischer (1994):} goal vs.\ instrument independence; Congress sets goals, Fed instrument-only.
    \item \textbf{Powell (2023):} ``operational, or instrument, independence, rather than goal independence.''
    \item \textbf{Dual mandate (\S225a):} tripartite; third dropped as redundant (Downey p.\ 15).
    \item \textbf{Kydland--Prescott (1977):} time-inconsistency; ``social objective function\ldots assumed to exist'' (p.\ 475).
    \item \textbf{Barro--Gordon (1983):} long-run neutrality; discretion $\rightarrow$ inflation bias.
    \item \textbf{Rogoff (1985):} conservative banker, ``greater weight on inflation stabilization'' (p.\ 1177); Romelli gloss p.\ 2.
    \item \textbf{Volcker 1979:} price-stability priority in practice w/o legal change; McNamara 2002 ``taken for granted\ldots little questioning'' (p.\ 47).
    \item \textbf{GMT (1991)/CWN (1992)/A\&S (1993):} indices; A\&S ``free lunch'' (lower inflation, no output cost).
    \item \textbf{Romelli (2022/24); Dincer et al.\ (2024); Garriga (2025):} same approach extended.
    \item Shared: de jure, time-inconsistency prior, price stability as benchmark, employment assumed away by construction.
    \item \textbf{Schnabel (2026):} dual mandate $\approx$ inconsequential in practice --- flag, develop 4.2.
    \item \textbf{Fischer (1995) technocratic claim:} complexity justifies insulation + pre-emptively delegitimises democratic goal-oversight.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How the Literature Shapes the Frame --- Epistemic Foreclosure (\textasciitilde450 words)}

\textit{Job: name what KIND of thing epistemic foreclosure is (ideas-shape-policy, not capture), define it, differentiate it, and show why only the economics literature percolated into the frame. [Main unwritten theory piece; now absorbs old 4.1.3.]}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Weiss (1979), enlightenment model:} research shapes policy not by instrumental use of specific findings but by generalizations/orientations that ``percolate\ldots through manifold indirect and unguided channels'' and reshape how problems are framed. $\leftarrow$ the mechanism for epistemic foreclosure. Also her downside: the process ``dispenses invalid as well as valid generalizations'' --- the conservative-banker default percolated as settled truth, normative content unexamined.
    \item \textbf{Epistemic foreclosure (the contribution):} goal folded into ``independence'' $\rightarrow$ within the frame the literature builds, contesting the goal = reducing independence; the question can't be posed. Literature-level, NOT political-outcome.
    \item \textbf{Zingales (2013): academic capture} --- nearest neighbour (career/funding/socialisation shape economists' conclusions). Differentiate: Zingales = \textit{why} economists produce pro-market findings; epistemic foreclosure = \textit{what the resulting frame forecloses}. Complementary, not the same.
    \item \textbf{Stigler (1971)/Dal B\'{o} (2006):} one line --- capture exists and includes MP, but this is NOT an acquisition-capture claim; mechanism is ideational (Weiss), not lobbying.
    \item \textbf{Non-interaction of the two literatures [was 4.1.3]:} econ $\leftrightarrow$ poli-sci don't cite each other (e.g.\ Romelli ignores poli-sci). Incommensurable questions: econ = \textit{optimise} MP; poli-sci = \textit{whose interests}. Only the economics frame percolated into policy (Weiss) --- which is why grounding the critique \textit{in} that literature (my contribution) matters. Bridges the two.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Epistemological Prior (\textasciitilde1,800 words)}
\label{sec:epistemology}

\textit{Step 1: the bias is encoded in the literature.}

\subsubsection{Conservative Central Banker (\textasciitilde1,060 words) [DRAFTED]}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Inflation is distributionally heterogeneous; models assume one objective function $\rightarrow$ erase conflict pre-analysis. Two heterogeneities: (a) poor's basket vs.\ aggregate; (b) creditor/debtor via maturity transformation. Posen p.\ 256 ``significant redistributive effects.''
    \item Price stability as unexamined optimum --- KP p.\ 480; BG acknowledge distortions (p.\ 103) then drop them, representative agent (p.\ 102).
    \item Rogoff banker: more inflation-averse than public; \textit{why}/how left unanswered.
    \item Posen/finance: finance more inflation-averse; ``uniquely positioned to provide that support'' (p.\ 254) [noted, not causal].
    \item \textbf{Wansleben complexity para [now in draft]:} modern MP innovations make expansionary policy non-harmful/beneficial to banks; simple conservative-banker models don't capture this --- pre-empts ``finance only benefits from tight money'' overclaim.
    \item QE complication (Adolph p.\ 738): finance $\neq$ uniform beneficiary of tight policy.
    \item ``Third-best'': Rogoff assumes employment suppressed by distortions (UI etc.) --- contestable, not neutral.
    \item Payoff: default is normatively loaded.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Empirical Evidence \& CBI Indices (\textasciitilde600 words) [DRAFTED]}
\begin{itemize}
    \item De jure vs.\ de facto; de jure chosen (assumptions explicit in coding).
    \item Conflation: indices measure mandate \textit{content}, not operational autonomy.
    \item Lineage: Bade-Parkin (1988) political/financial $\rightarrow$ Alesina (1988) demotes financial (``appears more relevant,'' p.\ 41) $\rightarrow$ GMT (1991) split ``final goal'' vs.\ ``instruments'' (p.\ 366) $\rightarrow$ \textbf{CWN (1992)} drops split, keeps objectives; ``In Rogoff's terminology, the objectives variable measures the strength of the `conservative bias''' (p.\ 357) $\leftarrow$ smoking gun; root of Romelli/Dincer/Garriga.
    \item A\&S (1993): ``promotes price stability\ldots no measurable impact on real economic performance'' (p.\ 151); folds ``price stability objective\ldots part of central bank statute'' (p.\ 153) into \textit{political} independence.
    \item Thought experiment: cut employment leg $\rightarrow$ US score rises, though goal + instrument independence untouched $\rightarrow$ circularity.
    \item \textbf{US/Turkey anomaly} (3 tables): Turkey higher on every index; gap in objectives (Romelli 0.250/1.000; Garriga 0.400/0.800). Turkey = Posen's lost-independence$\rightarrow$inflation case ($\rightarrow$ PIIE 2025, NOT 1995). 2001 IMF statute $\rightarrow$ high score, Erdo\u{g}an eroded substance $\rightarrow$ measure tracks goal-conformity.
    \item Eijffinger \& de Haan (2026): CWN ``considers the conservativeness\ldots as defined by law,'' ``should have been given much more weight'' (p.\ 4).
    \item Citation fact (NOT ``widely cited'' in text --- draft still says ``thousands''; replace): CWN \& A\&S $\sim$[N] cites each (Scholar, [date], separate); appear in recent speeches (Powell 2023; Kugler 2024).
    \item Conclusion: within the frame, contesting the goal = scored less independent. The foreclosure is in the apparatus itself.
    \item \textbf{Non-interaction of literatures [folded into 2.3]:} shown textually (Romelli ignores poli-sci); incommensurable questions (econ = optimise; poli-sci = whose interests). My critique bridges by attacking econ on its own terms.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion (\textasciitilde650 words)}

\begin{itemize}
    \item The circular loop restated: literature encodes the goal into the definition of independence $\rightarrow$ cited as the arrangement's warrant $\rightarrow$ epistemic foreclosure (the goal can't be posed within the frame).
    \item ``Ingrained'': internal to the legitimating framework from the start.
    \item \textbf{Contribution 1:} epistemic foreclosure as a mechanism --- the precise internal critique of the economics literature that poli-sci hasn't made.
    \item \textbf{Contribution 2:} CBI measurement is circular (legal goals, not instrument independence).
    \item Technocratic loop: expertise $\rightarrow$ insulation $\rightarrow$ goal unchallenged $\rightarrow$ validates expertise.
    \item \textbf{Why the goals matter (expand):} MP no longer neutral/limited (QE, facilities, forward guidance, regulation); long-run neutrality no longer holds $\rightarrow$ distributional force $\rightarrow$ Fed picks winners (finance)/losers (bottom). The economics literature supplies the justification for the market-as-prison Lindblom describes. The literature should be revised to make the buried normative-distributional decisions explicit.
    \item \textbf{The implication, flagged not claimed:} whether this translates into political foreclosure of democratic revision is a separate question requiring different evidence --- a future paper. This paper claims only the circularity \textit{within the literature}.
    \item \textbf{PROVOCATIVE CLOSE [to cook --- the one place I write provocatively]:} the goals of finance/MP \textit{could} target the poor; an inclusive financial system is, in my view, socially optimal. Imagine an MP regime and financial system that works for everyone --- and demand our institutions meet that image. [lore to expand]
    \item Scope honesty: about the literature + its transmission into discourse, not a finance conspiracy, not a democratic-failure claim.
    \item Caveat: NOT about removing Powell (authoritarianism = separate agenda).
    \item Close: the question the literature can't ask --- should the goal be different? --- is the one a democracy should be able to pose.
\end{itemize}
