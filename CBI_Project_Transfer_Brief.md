## PART 3 — SUBSTANTIVE ARGUMENT (state as submitted)

**Thesis.** The economics literature on CBI regularly conflates *goal* independence (choosing objectives) with *instrument* independence (choosing tools to hit given objectives). Theoretical models defending the current arrangement presuppose that the central bank's objective function should match the "conservative central banker" — more inflation-averse than the public. This encodes a normative-distributional choice into the *definition and measurement* of independence, while that same literature is cited as the warrant for the institutional arrangement. The result is a closed loop.

**Named contribution: epistemic foreclosure.** Defined **strictly at the literature level**. It is *not* a claim that democratic revision is politically impossible.

**Empirical spine — the index critique.** Alesina & Summers (1993), Cukierman et al. (1992), and the Romelli/Garriga/Dincer descendants build price-stability conservatism into the measurement of independence itself. Romelli's objectives sub-index penalises the Fed's dual mandate (US 0.25 vs. Türkiye 1.00, 2023). The de jure indices therefore score the US *below* Türkiye — while Posen's PIIE Briefing 25-3 Introduction cites Türkiye as a paradigm case of lost independence causing inflation. **This inconsistency between the qualitative narrative and the quantitative instrument is the methodological lever.**

**Verified quantitative results (do not re-derive from memory; recompute if challenged):**
- Romelli and Garriga overall indices are simple unweighted means of sub-indices.
- Dropping the objectives component cuts the Romelli US–Türkiye gap from 0.187 to 0.074, but *widens* Garriga's from 0.470 to 0.494.
- LVES carries 0.2 weight on objectives, accounting for at most 45% of the US–Türkiye LVES gap.
- **The objectives component does not explain the whole anomaly. Do not overclaim that it does.**

**"Acknowledged yet entrenched."** The mechanism is structural entrenchment, not bad faith. The strongest primary evidence:
- **Meyer (2000)** names the circularity and prefers Bade–Parkin *precisely because* it excludes mandate content — and the dominant index architecture was not dislodged. This failure is itself the evidence that the mechanism runs through institutional entrenchment rather than persuasion.
- **Fischer (2015)** names the dual-mandate penalty as a convention, then declines to apply it to the Fed.
- **Cukierman (2008)** fn. 6 uses "conservativeness" and "independence" interchangeably.
- **Kugler (2024)** fn. 20 explicitly names the Romelli index as penalising the dual mandate.
- **Bernanke (2004)** on giving the central banker an objective function differing from society's true preferences, and on academic ideas reshaping institutional design.

**Causal chain.** Runs through whichever index architecture became the *external operative benchmark* (the Cukierman-descended family: Romelli, Garriga, Dincer) — **not** through officials' personal beliefs.

**Standing scope rules.**
- Finance is **noted, not causal** throughout.
- **No hypocrisy framing** for Fed officials.
- "Epistemic foreclosure" is a literature-level claim only.
- The de facto independence subsection (Binder 2021) was **cut** for word budget. Binder is not in the draft body. If reintroduced: Binder (2021), *JMCB* 53(4), narrative coding of EIU/BMI reports, 118 central banks, 2010–2018; **key finding** that the pressure measure is uncorrelated with legal CBI indices (methodological support for the de jure/de facto gap); **tension** that pressure is almost always for looser policy and links to higher inflation (the orthodox story). Use as a methodological lever, never as an ally of the capture argument. Data: osf.io/kjcfh.
- Title history: prospectus was "Central Bank Independence and Regulatory Capture"; working titles included "Captured by Design" and "Assumptions Matter"; **final title is "Dependence on Assumptions."** Watch for stale titles in old notes.

**Forward research (not this dissertation).** Anat Admati on leverage vs. equity — banks expand liquidity through debt rather than capital; possible bridge to Wansleben's "liquidity illusion" (pp. 228–229), where thin equity requires constant refinancing, making the market-based funding infrastructure that central banks co-construct structurally necessary. Potential link between capital-structure choices and infrastructural capture. Relevant to Stanford PhD applications.
